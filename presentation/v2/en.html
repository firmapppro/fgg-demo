Our approach

FGG does not show the client the whole market.

The business aviation market is vast. A single route can have dozens of options — different in aircraft position, airports, preparation time, range, cabin, baggage and cost. Showing all of that market is a broker’s job. Ours is different.

We form our own selection of specific aircraft. A given aircraft inside FGG is no longer the same as an identical Citation XLS+ on the open market: we know the tail and the operator, we have studied the cabin, we understand its real condition and service capability, we have flown it, gathered feedback, and know which tasks it is good for.

We turn a vast aircraft market into a limited set of proven aviation solutions.

Cessna Citation XLS+

08:30. You are in Central Europe.

FGG identifies the nearest suitable departure airport and the airport most convenient to the meeting.

A long-range aircraft is unnecessary for a task like this. A specific 2016 Citation XLS+ that our team knows and that is available nearby lets you leave from a regional airport and shorten the total travel time.

We compare how well different trip scenarios work, not aircraft prices.

Price is transparent, but it is not the starting point of the choice. First the trip is defined. Then the suitable ways to complete it. The cost of each solution is clear in advance and is considered together with time, airports and comfort.

Scenario 02

A different trip — different priorities.

Evening in Dubai. Tomorrow: a family trip — six people, including children, substantial baggage, a long-range routing with no stop.

Cabin space, the ability to rest in flight, baggage volume and a nonstop routing come to the fore. This task needs an aircraft of a different class — not the one that covered a morning meeting in Europe.

Scenario 03

Bombardier Global Express

Evening in Europe. Tomorrow morning: negotiations on another continent.
Four on board. Work to be done in flight, with no stop.

A tight schedule, a long distance, and the need to arrive ready for the meeting. An ultra-long-range aircraft fits this task: a Global Express gives range, space to work and a nonstop flight — without a connection and without losing a day.

The difference is not aircraft class as status. The difference is the task in front of the trip — and which specific aircraft the team is prepared to recommend for it.

A specific aircraft

We do not select an aircraft type.
We select a specific aircraft.

A Mercedes S-Class is not one thing. A Four Seasons is not one thing. Two yachts of the same model can be in entirely different condition. It is the same with aircraft. A mass charter marketplace often hides that behind a photograph of a type. We do the opposite.

For us, a Citation XLS+ is not a category of aircraft. It is a specific aircraft we are prepared to send a client on.
